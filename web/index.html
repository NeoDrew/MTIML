How VC dimension upper-bounds Rademacher complexity

A self-contained walk-through of one of the most beautiful results in statistical learning theory — built up from population risk to a clean √(d log m / m) generalisation bound, with every proof spelled out.

The whole result, in one chain

Every chapter here serves to justify a single line of reasoning. Memorise this chain — every result on the route is a step in it.

generalisation gap ≤ 2 · Rademacher ≤ √(2 ln ΠF(m)/m) ≤ √(2d ln(em/d)/m)

From left to right: symmetrisation swaps an unknown population error for a quantity we can analyse; Massart's lemma turns finite label-vector sets into a square-root entropy bound; Sauer's lemma says VC dimension forces polynomial growth where 2m is exponential. Compose the steps and you get uniform convergence.

Who this is for

Anyone who has seen population risk and empirical risk before, but is fuzzy on why finite VC dimension implies a learning algorithm generalises. We start from definitions, do every proof in full, and finish with a generalisation theorem that beats the trivial 2m bound.

How to read this site

Read in order. Each chapter assumes the previous ones. There are about 30–40 minutes of reading per session if you work through the proofs by hand — try to redo the algebra on paper as you go, especially the four core proofs:

  • Massart's lemma (Chapter 5) — Jensen + MGF + optimise λ.
  • Halfspaces have VCdim d+1 (Chapter 8) — explicit shattering + Radon.
  • Radon's lemma (Chapter 8) — under-determined linear system.
  • Sauer's lemma (Chapter 9) — induction on m+d with the F2/F3 split.
