Section 4

Massart's Finite Class Lemma

A square-root entropy bound on Rademacher complexity for any finite set.

Theorem

Theorem (Massart)

Let $\mathcal A \subset \mathbb R^m$ be finite and $r = \sup_{a \in \mathcal A}\|a\|_2$. Then $$\mathbb E_{\varepsilon}\!\left[\sup_{a \in \mathcal A}\,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m \varepsilon_i a_i\right] \;\le\; \frac{r\sqrt{2 \ln |\mathcal A|}}{m}.$$

Proof sketch

Proof.

With $\mu = \mathbb E_{\varepsilon}\bigl[\sup_{a \in \mathcal A}\sum_i \varepsilon_i a_i\bigr]$, Jensen's inequality and bounding the supremum by a sum yield, for any $\lambda > 0$, $$e^{\lambda \mu} \;\le\; \sum_{a \in \mathcal A}\,\prod_{i=1}^m \mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda \varepsilon_i a_i}\right].$$

Since $\mathbb E[e^{\lambda \varepsilon_i a_i}] = \cosh(\lambda a_i) \le e^{\lambda^2 a_i^2 / 2}$, this gives $$e^{\lambda \mu} \;\le\; |\mathcal A|\, e^{\lambda^2 r^2 / 2}.$$

Taking logs and dividing by $\lambda$: $$\mu \;\le\; \frac{\ln|\mathcal A|}{\lambda} + \frac{\lambda r^2}{2}.$$

Minimising in $\lambda$ at $\lambda = \sqrt{2\ln|\mathcal A|}/r$ gives $\mu \le r\sqrt{2\ln|\mathcal A|}$. Dividing by $m$ produces the stated bound.

Application to $\mathcal F$

Setting $\mathcal A = \mathcal F\big|_{x_1^m}$ and noting $\|a\|_2 = \sqrt m$ for any $a \in \{\pm 1\}^m$:

$$\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal F) \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt m \cdot \sqrt{2 \ln \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)}}{m} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{2 \ln \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)}{m}}.$$

So Rademacher complexity of any binary class is bounded by the log of its growth function. The trivial bound $\Pi_{\mathcal F}(m) \le 2^m$ gives a non-vanishing $\sqrt{2\ln 2}$ — the next sections show how VC dimension brings this down to something that decays with $m$.

Critical points

What this lemma actually says
  1. Sub-Gaussian concentration. The bound $\cosh(\lambda a) \le e^{\lambda^2 a^2 / 2}$ encodes that $\varepsilon a$ is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy $a^2$; this is the same machinery underlying Hoeffding's inequality.
  2. Origin of the $\sqrt{\log |\mathcal A|}$ factor. It arises from replacing the supremum with a sum, effectively a union bound. Tightening it requires chaining arguments (Dudley's entropy integral).
  3. Universality. The lemma applies to any finite subset of $\mathbb R^m$; its application to function classes is one instance of a general tool.

What's next

We now have $\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal F) \le \sqrt{2 \ln \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m) / m}$. To turn this into a useful generalisation guarantee we need $\Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)$ to grow slowly. The next section introduces the right structural property — VC dimension — and Section 6 (Sauer's lemma) delivers the polynomial growth bound.
