Section 7

From VC Dimension to Generalisation

Composing the chain.

The composed bound

Combining Massart's Lemma with Sauer's Lemma, for any binary class $\mathcal F$ with $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal F) \le d$ and $m \ge d$:

Generalisation bound

$$\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal F) \;\le\; \sqrt{\frac{2 \ln \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)}{m}} \;\le\; \sqrt{\frac{2 d \ln(em/d)}{m}}.$$

Dichotomy of growth

If $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal F) = \infty$, then $\Pi_{\mathcal F}(m) = 2^m$ for every $m$. Otherwise, Sauer's Lemma implies polynomial growth: $$\Pi_{\mathcal F}(m) = O(m^d).$$ No intermediate growth rate occurs.

Two regimes only: full exponential or eventually polynomial. Nothing in between.

Interpretation

Finite VC dimension prevents the hypothesis class from sustaining exponential combinatorial growth.

Consequence

For $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal F) = d < \infty$, $$\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal F) \;=\; O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{d \ln m}{m}}\right) \;\to\; 0\ \text{as}\ m \to \infty.$$ Consequently, the expected gap between empirical and true performance vanishes with more data.

The whole chain in one image

finite VC dimension ↓ Sauer polynomial growth ↓ Massart small Rademacher complexity ↓ symmetrisation generalisation
The headline

Finite VC dimension, a purely combinatorial property, is sufficient to guarantee learnability independently of the underlying data distribution.
