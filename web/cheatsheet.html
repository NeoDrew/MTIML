Cheat-sheet & Q&A

Everything on one page; common viva questions with model answers.

The chain in one image

$\mathbb E\sup(\widehat R - R)$ ≤ $2\,\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal F)$ ≤ $\sqrt{\tfrac{2\ln \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)}{m}}$ ≤ $\sqrt{\tfrac{2d\ln(em/d)}{m}}$

Definitions you should know cold

  • Population risk: $R(f) = \mathbb E_{(x,y)}[\ell(y, f(x))]$.
  • Empirical risk: $\widehat R(f) = \tfrac{1}{m}\sum_i \ell(y_i, f(x_i))$.
  • Rademacher complexity: $\mathfrak R_m(\mathcal H) = \mathbb E_{S,\varepsilon}\bigl[\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}\tfrac1m\sum \varepsilon_i h(z_i)\bigr]$, $\varepsilon_i$ i.i.d. $\pm 1$.
  • Restriction: $\mathcal F\big|_{x_1^m} = \{(f(x_1),\dots,f(x_m)) : f\in\mathcal F\}\subseteq\{\pm 1\}^m$.
  • Growth function: $\Pi_{\mathcal F}(m) = \max_{x_1^m}|\mathcal F\big|_{x_1^m}|$.
  • Shattering: $\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}$ is shattered if $\mathcal F\big|_{x_1^m}=\{\pm 1\}^m$.
  • VC dimension: $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal F) = \max\{m : \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m) = 2^m\}$, equivalently the size of the largest shattered set.

Proofs you should be able to reconstruct

The four core proofs
  1. Massart's lemma. Jensen on $e^{\lambda \mu}$ → sup ≤ sum → independence + cosh bound → optimise $\lambda = \sqrt{2\ln|\mathcal A|}/r$.
  2. Halfspace lower bound. Shatter $\{0, e_1, \dots, e_d\}$ via $\theta = -b_0$, $w_i = \theta + b_i$.
  3. Radon's lemma. Underdetermined system $\sum\lambda_i x_i = 0, \sum \lambda_i = 0$. Split by sign of $\lambda^*_i$. Common convex combination.
  4. Sauer's lemma. Induction on $m+d$. $|\mathcal F_1| = |\mathcal F_2| + |\mathcal F_3|$. $\mathcal F_3$ has VCdim $\le d-1$. Pascal.

Worked numbers to memorise

  • Trivial Rademacher bound: $\sqrt{2\ln 2} \approx 1.18$ — useless.
  • Halfspaces in $\mathbb R^d$: VCdim $= d+1$ ⇒ bound $\sim \sqrt{(d+1)\ln(em/(d+1)) / m}$.
  • Sample complexity: $m = \tilde O(d/\varepsilon^2)$ to achieve generalisation gap $\varepsilon$.
  • Convex polygons in $\mathbb R^2$ (no vertex limit): VCdim $= \infty$ ⇒ no uniform convergence.

Q&A drills

Q1. Why is Rademacher complexity the right complexity measure?

Three reasons. (i) Symmetrisation says it's an upper bound on the generalisation gap, so bounding it suffices. (ii) It is distribution-aware — unlike VC, it depends on $D$, so it can be tighter. (iii) It composes well: contraction shows that Lipschitz losses don't blow up Rademacher by more than a constant factor.

Q2. Why does the bound have a square root?

Massart's lemma. The proof minimises $\ln|\mathcal A|/\lambda + \lambda r^2/2$ over $\lambda$. The optimum is at $\lambda \propto \sqrt{\ln|\mathcal A|}$, giving an optimal value $\propto r\sqrt{\ln|\mathcal A|}$. The square root is the signature of sub-Gaussian concentration — anything that looks like a maximum-of-Gaussians has a $\sqrt{\ln N}$ scaling.

Q3. Where does VC dimension actually enter the chain?

Only at one step: Sauer's lemma. Everything before (symmetrisation, Massart, the Theorem 1.1 application) is general. VC dimension is what bounds $\Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)$ by a polynomial in $m$, which is what makes the final bound shrink as $m \to \infty$.

Q4. Why does Radon's lemma need exactly $d+2$ points, not $d+1$?

The system has $d+1$ equations: $d$ "linear" (one per coordinate of $\mathbb R^d$) plus $1$ "affine" ($\sum \lambda_i = 0$). To guarantee a non-trivial solution we need strictly more unknowns than equations: $d + 2$. With only $d+1$ points the system might admit only the zero solution.

Q5. Why is the affine constraint $\sum \lambda_i = 0$ in Radon's lemma?

It forces both signs to appear among the $\lambda_i$. Without it, all $\lambda_i$ might be positive, and we couldn't split them into a non-empty $P$ and a non-empty $N$. The affine constraint is exactly what guarantees the partition.

Q6. What's an example of a class with infinite VC dimension?

Convex polygons in $\mathbb R^2$ (no vertex bound). Take $m$ points on a circle. For any subset, draw a polygon with vertices placed just outside that subset and faces cutting between the chosen and unchosen points. Every $2^m$ labelling is achievable, for every $m$. Hence VCdim $= \infty$.

Q7. How does this relate to deep learning?

Modern neural networks have VC dimension that scales with the number of parameters, which is enormous — often greater than $m$. Our VC bound is then vacuous. This is exactly the puzzle posed by Zhang et al. ("Understanding deep learning requires rethinking generalization", 2017) — they showed nets can fit random labels yet still generalise on real data. Resolving this requires bounds based on margins, optimisation dynamics, or the algorithm itself, not just the class.

Q8. Is finite VC dimension necessary for PAC learning?

Yes — for binary classification under 0–1 loss, the fundamental theorem of statistical learning says finite VC ⇔ uniform convergence ⇔ PAC learnable (agnostic or realisable) ⇔ ERM works. Infinite VC ⇒ no algorithm can succeed in the worst case, by a no-free-lunch argument.

Q9. Why ±1 instead of $\{0, 1\}$?

Pure convention; both are used. The $\pm 1$ encoding makes the algebra around Rademacher signs cleaner (e.g. $1[y \ne y'] = (1 - yy')/2$ is symmetric). Sauer's lemma and VC dim are unchanged because the structure of the labelling lattice $\{0,1\}^m$ and $\{\pm1\}^m$ are isomorphic.

Q10. What can break the entire chain?

Three things, listed by likelihood: (i) the class is infinite-VC, so Sauer offers no help; (ii) the loss is not bounded or not Lipschitz, breaking Massart and contraction; (iii) data is not i.i.d., breaking symmetrisation. Any of these requires a different theory — VC bounds are specifically about i.i.d. binary classification with bounded losses.

"In the room" cheat lines

Lines you can drop verbatim during a Q&A and sound competent:

  • "Rademacher complexity captures whether the class can fit pure $\pm 1$ noise — that is the worst case for overfitting."
  • "The factor of two in the master theorem comes from splitting a triangle inequality after introducing the ghost sample."
  • "The cosh-of-Rademacher inequality is the only place randomness actually enters Massart's proof — everything else is exponential algebra."
  • "Sauer's lemma gives the dichotomy: the growth function is either always $2^m$ or eventually polynomial. Nothing in between."
  • "The $+1$ in halfspace VC dim is the bias; without it, homogeneous halfspaces have VC dim $d$."
  • "The bound is distribution-free, which is its strength and its limitation: it works for every $D$ but is too pessimistic for any specific $D$."

Last reminders before you walk in

  • Memorise the chain. If you forget anything else, you can rebuild from there.
  • Practice Massart's proof on paper without notes. It's the proof most likely to be asked.
  • For Radon, draw the picture: $d+2$ points in $\mathbb R^d$, two convex hulls intersecting at a common point.
  • Know the dichotomy: $2^m$ vs $m^d$. Sauer is the bridge.
  • If asked something off-topic, fall back on the chain — most questions reduce to one of its links.
