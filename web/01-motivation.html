Section 1

Motivation: The Generalisation Gap

Why training error isn't enough.

★ Drew's section to present

This is one of Drew's three sections (Intro + §1 + §2). The presenter view has the spoken script.

Empirical risk vs population risk

Statistical learning theory addresses the discrepancy between empirical risk, observed on training data, and population risk, which governs out-of-sample performance: $$\widehat R(f) = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m \ell(y_i, f(x_i)),\qquad R(f) = \mathbb E[\ell(y, f(x))].$$ The empirical risk is computable from the sample. The population risk is not — it depends on the unknown data distribution $D$. The whole game is to use the former as a proxy for the latter.

The generalisation gap

Definition

The generalisation gap for a fixed $f$ is $\Delta_f = \widehat R(f) - R(f)$. Since $f$ is selected from $\mathcal F$ on the basis of the sample, the relevant quantity is the worst-case gap over the class: $$\sup_{f \in \mathcal F}\bigl(\widehat R(f) - R(f)\bigr).$$ A uniform bound on this supremum underpins distribution-free generalisation guarantees.

Why "uniform"? If you only know $\widehat R(f) \approx R(f)$ for a single fixed $f$, you can't say anything about the $f$ your algorithm chose after looking at the data. Empirical risk minimisation (ERM) returns a function that depends on the sample, so we need the bound to hold simultaneously over every $f \in \mathcal F$.

The picture

A class that's too small can't fit the data; a class that's too large memorises it. The vertical gap between the curves is what we will bound.

The driving question

Goal

The objective is to ensure empirical risk approximates true risk uniformly over $\mathcal F$. That is, find a bound on $$\mathbb E_S\!\left[\sup_{f \in \mathcal F}\bigl(\widehat R(f) - R(f)\bigr)\right]$$ that goes to $0$ as $m \to \infty$, and that depends on $\mathcal F$ only through some manageable structural quantity.

The next sections show that the "manageable structural quantity" turns out to be the VC dimension, and that the route from there back to the generalisation gap passes through Rademacher complexity, the growth function, and Massart's and Sauer's lemmas.
