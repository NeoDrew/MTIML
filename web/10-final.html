Chapter 10

The final chain

VC dimension upper-bounds Rademacher complexity, which upper-bounds the generalisation gap.

Putting it all together

We now compose every result of the previous nine chapters. The argument runs from "I want to bound the generalisation gap" to "$O(\sqrt{d\ln m / m})$" without a single black box.

Generalisation theorem

Let $\mathcal F \subseteq \{\pm 1\}^{\mathcal X}$ be a class of binary classifiers with $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal F) = d$. Then for $m \ge d$, $$ \mathbb{E}_S\!\left[\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\bigl(\widehat R(f) - R(f)\bigr)\right] \;\le\; 2\sqrt{\frac{2 d \ln(em/d)}{m}}. $$

Reading the chain

From left to right, line by line, with the chapter providing each step:

$$ \mathbb{E}_S\!\left[\sup_{f}\bigl(\widehat R(f) - R(f)\bigr)\right] $$

↓ symmetrisation (Ch. 3)

$$ \le\; 2\,\mathfrak R_m(\phi_{\mathcal F}) \;=\; \mathfrak R_m(\mathcal F) $$

↓ loss-class identity (Ch. 2 lemma)

$$ \le\; \sqrt{\frac{2 \ln \Pi_{\mathcal F}(m)}{m}} $$

↓ Massart + restriction (Ch. 5–6)

$$ \le\; \sqrt{\frac{2 \ln (em/d)^d}{m}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{2 d \ln(em/d)}{m}} $$

↓ Sauer's lemma + Corollary 3.1 (Ch. 9)

(Multiply by $2$ for the symmetrisation factor and you get the boxed bound. The factor-of-$2$ matters for the constants but not for the asymptotics.)

gap ≤ 2·Rademacher ≤ √(ln Π / m) ≤ √(d ln(em/d) / m)

What this says

Every line of the chain is doing real work. Removing any one breaks the result.

  • Symmetrisation connects an unobservable quantity (population risk) to one we can analyse with combinatorial tools (Rademacher complexity).
  • Massart is what makes Rademacher complexity meaningful — without it, "expected supremum over random signs" is just an expression.
  • Sauer is the surprise: a class is either fully expressive forever, or polynomial in growth past a threshold. There is no middle ground.

The bound's behaviour

$\sqrt{d\ln(em/d)/m}$ as a function of $m$, for several values of $d$. All three curves go to $0$, but classes with smaller VC dim need fewer samples to get there.

Three immediate consequences:

  • $d \to \infty$: the bound becomes vacuous. Classes with infinite VC dimension cannot be uniformly learnt without further structure (e.g. margins, sparsity).
  • $m \to \infty$ with $d$ fixed: the bound shrinks as $\sqrt{\ln m / m}$ — almost like $1/\sqrt m$, the parametric rate.
  • Sample complexity: to drive the bound below $\varepsilon$, it suffices to take $m = \tilde O(d / \varepsilon^2)$ samples (modulo log factors). This is tight up to logs — there are matching lower bounds.

From in-expectation to high-probability

The bound we proved is in expectation. Standard concentration arguments (e.g. McDiarmid's inequality) upgrade it to a high-probability statement. With probability at least $1 - \delta$ over the sample,

$$ \sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\bigl(\widehat R(f) - R(f)\bigr) \;\le\; 2\sqrt{\frac{2 d \ln(em/d)}{m}} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{2m}}. $$

The first term is what we just derived; the second comes from concentration of the supremum around its expectation.

From the gap to PAC learning

Combine the high-probability bound with ERM. If $\widehat f$ is the ERM classifier and $f^*$ is the best classifier in $\mathcal F$,

$$ R(\widehat f) - R(f^*) \le 2\sup_f|\widehat R(f) - R(f)| \;=\; \tilde O\!\left(\sqrt{d/m}\right). $$

So with $m = \tilde O(d/\varepsilon^2)$ samples, ERM achieves population risk within $\varepsilon$ of the best classifier in $\mathcal F$. This is the agnostic PAC learnability of any class with finite VC dimension.

The fundamental theorem of statistical learning

The big result that surrounds everything we did:

Fundamental theorem of statistical learning

For binary classification with the 0–1 loss, the following are equivalent:

  1. $\mathcal F$ has finite VC dimension.
  2. $\mathcal F$ has uniform convergence: $\sup_f|\widehat R(f) - R(f)| \to 0$ in probability as $m \to \infty$.
  3. $\mathcal F$ is agnostic PAC learnable.
  4. $\mathcal F$ is PAC learnable in the realisable case.
  5. ERM is a successful PAC learner for $\mathcal F$.

We've proved (1) ⇒ (2) ⇒ (3). The other direction — that infinite VC dimension implies no learner can succeed — is the harder half, called the no-free-lunch argument.

What this doesn't say (and where modern theory goes)

Our bound is distribution-free: it works for every distribution $D$, but with the same constants. That is too pessimistic in practice.

  • Modern deep networks have $\mathrm{VCdim} \gg m$ in nearly every realistic regime, yet generalise. The VC bound is vacuous for them.
  • Margin-based bounds replace the combinatorial $d$ with a continuous margin parameter that captures the geometry of the data. They give non-vacuous bounds for some neural nets.
  • Algorithmic stability reasons about the optimiser, not just the class — and bypasses VC entirely. The data-dependent and algorithm-dependent flavour of bounds is where the field has moved.
  • PAC-Bayes bounds, Rademacher complexity of the actual learnt model, and compression-based bounds are all alternative routes that handle modern over-parameterised regimes.

None of this diminishes what we did. The VC bound is the foundational, distribution-free, algorithm-agnostic guarantee — the result that everything else is benchmarked against.
