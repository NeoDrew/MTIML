Details of Coursework
The task for the coursework is that you will divide yourselves into groups of 3 and pick a reference (related to the lectures but in topics not covered in them) to read and present in detail as a group. Everyone in the group gets the same score --- the poster coursework is a group project. You will prepare and submit a poster and do an associated 15 minute poster presentation, and prepare to answer questions based on the paper/poster.

The poster, the oral presentation and the Q&A session, all 3 will be graded.
The presentation material is your choice.

You might want to use generative-AI to help with the coursework,
but if you do so, you are necessarily required to declare its usage. 

Note the set of instructions numbered A to H that follow.
And at the end of this page we have given a list of suggestions but you can pick your own - just check with us first.


A.
You are responsible for finding your own team, and notifying us of the members. 
Notification deadline is 27th March i.e the last Friday before Easter Break by when you should let us know as to who is your group of 3 and what are you preparing the poster on. So, you have ~2 months from the start of the course to decide on this outline plan. One of your group members needs to send me (anirbit.mukherjee@manchester.ac.uk) an email with the details, with cc to other group members with an email subject title of [COMP 34312 Poster Details].


B.
The poster session will be 10 AM - 12 PM and then 1PM - 2PM on 8th May with a reserve day of 11th May at the same times, if we cannot fit everyone on the first option. You are advised to come before time and set up the poster.

So, you have ~3 months from the start of the course to prepare your poster. This is not at all a long time to prepare given what is expected of a good poster (see the point E) and hence, you are advised to read ahead and start preparing immediately.

C.
Submission will require a PDF of the poster, apart from the in-person on a screen presentation explained above.


D.
The rooms Collab 1 and 2 have 12 setups of a large screen with a desk with several chairs. For their presentation, each group will be assigned a time slot and one of these setups. They would project their poster onto the screen and present it to the marker assigned to that session.
    
In any one round, there would be at most 6 groups presenting simultaneously at their assigned alternate setups spread across Collab 1 and 2. The markers will be the lecturers and some of the GTAs. We anticipate requiring not more than 6 rounds, each with at most 6 presentations and each round should take not more than 30 minutes.

H.
Options for the Poster Coursework

- Whenever a theorem/lemma number is mentioned as a suggestion its expected that its proof will be an integral part of the eventual presentation.

- Whenever a proof is presented, it is expected that proofs of all results invoked inside it are also to presented.

- The suggested references span a range of technical depth.
   To help you choose a topic we have done a 3-way labelling of the references, 
[CORE]
These could easily have been covered in a few additional lecture sessions in this course.

[INTERMEDIATE]
They are related to the lecture topics and start to introduce slightly more involved ideas than what has been taught.

[CHALLENGING] 
To understand these, you’ll need to learn a significantly new concept beyond the lectures.

- Selecting a CHALLENGING reference will not guarantee higher grades, just as choosing a CORE marked option will not restrict what grades you can achieve. No reference is inherently better than another; they simply differ in focus.
